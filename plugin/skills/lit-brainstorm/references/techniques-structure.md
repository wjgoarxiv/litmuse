# Structuring techniques

## Morphological box

For solutions with separable dimensions.

1. List 4 to 6 dimensions of any answer (who acts, when, where it runs, what triggers it, how it is delivered, how it is paid for).
2. List 3 to 5 options per dimension, including odd ones.
3. Draw the grid. Pick combinations: three at random, two the obvious way, two deliberately contrasting. Each combination is an idea `[morph:A2-B1-C3]`.
4. Mark combinations that are impossible; keep them visible with the reason.

| Dimension | Opt 1 | Opt 2 | Opt 3 | Opt 4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | manual | schedule | event | prediction |
| Where | local | server | edge | user device |

## Persona lenses

Pick three to five personas that differ in goal, skill and context (novice, expert, skeptic, accessibility needs, someone in a hurry, an operator who must support it). For each, write two ideas from their point of view and one thing they would refuse. Label `[persona:novice]`.

## Six hats

One pass per viewpoint, one to three ideas each:
- White: what data would unlock options?
- Red: what would feel good or bad to use?
- Black: what would a careful critic fear? (Idea form: how to neutralise it.)
- Yellow: where is the largest upside?
- Green: wild alternatives.
- Blue: how to organise the work.

Black-hat output stays idea-shaped (a mitigation or an alternative), not a verdict.

## Role storming

Name three roles with an interest in the problem (a customer, a maintainer, a competitor). Brainstorm as each, in first person: "If I were the competitor, I would...". Roles that conflict are useful; label `[role:competitor]`.
