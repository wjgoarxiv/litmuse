# Lenses

Each idea agent looks at the topic through exactly one lens. Agent `i` takes lens `i mod 32`, so neighbouring agents
always differ, and a run of 100 agents uses every lens three or four times with different seeds. Pass the lines below
to the Workflow `args.lenses` in this order, one string each (the text after the number).

1. The newcomer: someone meeting this for the first time, with no habits to protect.
2. The expert who is bored: what would surprise a person who has seen every usual answer?
3. Remove the main resource: the same goal with no budget, no team or no time.
4. Ten times the scale: what breaks, and what becomes possible, when everything is ten times bigger?
5. One tenth the scale: the smallest version that still delivers the core value.
6. Borrow from nature: a living system that solves a similar problem.
7. Borrow from another industry: how logistics, games, medicine or retail would handle it.
8. Invert the goal: how to make the problem worse, then reverse each move.
9. The hostile user: someone trying to misuse, cheat or break it.
10. The regulator: what a careful rule-maker would require, and the design that makes the rule easy.
11. The craftsperson: quality and feel over feature count.
12. Remove a step: the same outcome with one whole stage of today's process gone.
13. Swap the order: do the last step first.
14. Make it social: what changes when other people see, join or compete?
15. Make it private: what changes when nobody else ever sees it?
16. Automate the dull part: the piece a person hates doing, done by a machine.
17. Hand it to the person: the piece a machine does badly, given back to a human with good tools.
18. The time traveller from twenty years ahead: what will look obviously wrong about today's approach?
19. The historian: an old, forgotten way of solving this that could work again.
20. Price it differently: free, subscription, one-off, pay per result, or paid by someone else.
21. Change the channel: physical, voice, text, ambient, scheduled, on demand.
22. Constraint as feature: turn the most annoying limitation into the selling point.
23. The minimal promise: one sentence the user would repeat to a friend, then only what keeps it true.
24. Combine two unrelated ideas from this field into one.
25. The failure story: it shipped and failed a year later; what was the reason, and how is that designed out?
26. The accessibility lens: someone with low vision, one free hand, or little time.
27. The local lens: one city, one language, one community, done deeply.
28. The platform lens: something others build on instead of a finished product.
29. The measurement lens: what would make success visible every day?
30. The ethical lens: who could be harmed, and the version that harms no one.
31. Random stimulus: pick an object in your seed's position of the alphabet (A apple, B bridge, ...) and connect it.
32. The contrarian: the answer most people in the field would reject at first, argued well.
